① ONE BIT, CHOSEN IN TIME There is no neutral: the hull is always sinking or always rising. You toggle the valve bit — HOLD = VENT (−2.6 m/s vertical), RELEASE = FLOOD (+2.3 m/s) — and you choose when. Horizontal motion is never yours; you only choose which current carries you.
② SHEAR, DRAWN HONESTLY The flow is the curl of one streamfunction Ψ: exactly divergence-free — no sources, no sinks. Three bands carry you: +3.0 m/s east (0–25 m), +0.6 (25–55 m), −1.1 m/s west (55–130 m). Ten seeded eddies curl through the bands and are the only vertical motion in the sea. Gyres are geometry here, not decoration.
③ BUDGET = CERTIFIED K + 2 At every leg a real layered BFS runs in this page over (cell × valve-bit) — using the same fixed-step integrator the vehicle runs — and certifies the minimum valve shifts K needed to reach the beacon. Your grant is K + 2. Spend it and the listening station retires the leg: BEACON LOST. PLOT COURSE prints the solver's own plan as a dashed line with one red ring per shift.
④ THREE WAYS TO RETIRE BROACHED — z < 3 m: venting carelessly near the surface pops you out of the sea. CRUSHED — z ≥ 96 m: the counterflow's best repositioning water lies below the hull's contour. GROUNDED — the chart's shelf pinnacles rise to 49–71 m, exactly where a lazy descent lands.
⑤ THE WATCH IS THE GAME Between shifts: twenty to forty seconds of pure instrument-reading — the trace, the dashed plan, the beacon's echo. That is where anticipation lives: every toggle was cast seconds before its payoff, and everything since is its slow, deterministic maturation. There is no pause; the ocean does not care.
⑥ DEAD-RECKONING · THE PLOT CONE Arm DEAD-RECKONING and the chart stops plotting the hull and starts plotting the hull's reputation: an expanding stippled uncertainty ellipse grown from band-only dead reckoning — the plotter's estimator knows no eddies. The cone widens fastest while the hull is crossing the thermocline toll band. Crossing a sharp thermocline — or spending a rationed SONAR FIX (three per leg) — collapses the cone back to a crisp red stamped crosshair. Toggles are now scheduled against the cone's growth as well as the field.

① The machine owns the clock Treadle (3 picks) and card (4 picks) turn together; the shed — the clean window — opens only at their coincidence, two picks in twelve. You can change neither the pattern, the cadence, nor the colour cards. You are not the designer of this tapestry.
② One mallet, four grades Clean catch — tangle, shed open: no trace. Hurry catch — tangle, shed closed: it clears, but a dark slack row is woven. Clean splice — a dye bobbin runs dry and you strike: the loom blends its two tray neighbours, no defect. Blind mallet — nothing pending: a slack row, for nothing. Do nothing, most of the time, on purpose.
③ Tangles are fair by construction Spawns come from a hash of (seed, card, pick), and only when the next shed is 4–9 picks away: every tangle offers a shed window before it can be trapped. Width grows on its 3rd, 6th and 9th unstruck pick; a shuttle can only snag it on a shed-closed pick — so the last-gated tangle is caught as the window opens, or snaps the moment it closes. A tear leaves a bare column in the selvedge forever. Three tears and the shop frames the cloth. (Measured: diligent shed-attendance = 0 tears over 40 seeds × 250 picks.)
④ The loom decides the colourway When a bobbin dries, its two neighbours blend, equally, in the tray's mechanical order — forever. Your only art is when the splice happens; a forced splice (you never struck) weaves two pale slub rows. The tapestry remembers every blend.
⑤ The blends ledger The sample book registers each irreversible blend. Watch the palette drift down the page — that drift is the history of your timing.
⑥ The cloth is the fault schedule At any checkpoint — or on framing — MOUNT CLOTH · GEN II+ hashes the woven tapestry itself into the next punch-card chain: slack picks raise the spawn pressure (a slack-heavy cloth is a tangle-rich cloth); tear scars imprint their warp columns as scar heddles where new tangles prefer to live; two scars even accelerate maturation; dye splices shorten every next bobbin and the final blend carries over as the heirloom ink. Faults are hashed with a salt taken from the cloth, so the selvage is a seed: replay-by-cloth reproduces the run. EXPORT SPECIMEN (SVG) prints the current weave as a standalone Meiji sample sheet — kentō marks, hanko, seed hash, every pick.
